How to make a long distance relationship work across time zones

All guidesMagpie

Distance is hard; distance plus a time difference is harder. When one of you is waking up as the other goes to bed, it's easy to feel out of sync. These habits help couples stay close when their clocks never match.

Find your overlap

  1. Write down both your days hour by hour and circle the windows when you're both awake and free
  2. Protect one shared window as a fixed daily appointment, even if it's only 15 minutes
  3. Use the other windows for messages, not calls
  4. Check the clock change dates: the gap can move by an hour twice a year

Make asynchronous feel close

  1. Leave a voice note for their morning
  2. Send a photo of your evening for them to wake up to
  3. Keep a shared note or list you both add to through the day
  4. Answer a daily question each, then read both answers together
  5. Send a small “I'm thinking of you” signal that doesn't need a reply

Keep it fair

  1. Take turns being the one who stays up late or wakes up early
  2. Don't let one person always adapt to the other's schedule
  3. Agree on when it's okay to not answer (work, sleep, friends)
  4. Talk about it when it starts to feel unbalanced

Protect your sleep

  1. Avoid daily calls that cut into sleep: tiredness makes every conversation harder
  2. Use weekends for the long calls
  3. Agree on a goodnight ritual that works at any hour

Tips

  • Show both clocks somewhere you look every day, so you always know what their day looks like.
  • Plan the next reunion early: a date on the calendar makes the time difference easier to live with.
